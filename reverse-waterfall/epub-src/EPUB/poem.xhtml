Reverse Waterfall

When an animal wakes from its dream, it becomes human. What animals do not know is that humans, too, wish to wake from the dream and become something else—that just as whales suffer from hemophilia, humans are afflicted by imagined orders—that their thoughts, seeping drop by drop from a living body into the sky, will not clot.

By the time I came to my senses, I had already hit “Agree to All” on the Terms of Service for humankind’s official app. I looked back at Ararat, where the Ark had run aground, lit a stick of incense, and sneezed. Raindrops sprayed from the apex of the three-thousandfold world. When the rain stopped, humans spread like bad breath—the shabby lineage of vertebrates, dressed up in a fine name.

I have another memory like this. Nothing seemed as lovely as moss clinging to a rock, you said. Below us, a thunderstorm was crossing the jungles of Kalimantan. At eight thousand feet, inside a shelter of reinforced glass, we waited for the coffee water to boil. I want to go to a world where it rains forever, you said. Across the surface of the earth, humans built iridescent houses.

In one possible future, the glow of awakening spread throughout the nervous system. When day broke, the waterfall poured upward, and everything returned to the very beginning. Our descendants had come to prune a past that had grown crooked. Gently they woke us, with no need at all for hatred, saying—

it’s morning,
it’s morning.
